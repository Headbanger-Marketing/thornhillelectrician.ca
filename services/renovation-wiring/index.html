Renovation wiring in Thornhill.

Renovating a Thornhill house one room at a time means the wiring plan has to work for finished rooms and open walls at once. Each stage needs a clear end point so occupied rooms remain in use.

Electrical decisions belong early in the renovation

A renovation changes how the space will be used and what equipment it must serve. Provide the drawings and appliance specifications before the electrical scope is finalized. A Licensed Electrical Contractor can assess retained wiring, new circuits and the proposed loads. Municipal building permissions and ESA electrical notification are separate matters, and the project may require both.

Prepare the scope for your Thornhill property

List the rooms in the order they will be renovated, and mark which ones the household will sleep, cook and wash in during each stage. Collect any drawings and note the other trades who will open walls.

Ask the contractor how circuits serving finished rooms will be kept intact when a neighbouring wall is opened. Ask whether the ESA notification covers every stage or each one separately.

What should the assessment establish?

Each stage of the renovation gets its own list from the contractor of existing wiring retained and wiring replaced. The contractor also shows how circuits feeding rooms still in use are separated from those in the work area of the Thornhill renovation, and explains what the household can plug in and switch on between stages. The scope lists every stage with its start and end condition, the other trades involved and the arrangements for the ESA notification. Responsibility for labelling the circuits as the work proceeds is assigned to a named person in the scope.

Ontario electrical safety

ESA says almost all electrical work needs a notification of work. If you hire someone for home electrical work, use a Licensed Electrical Contractor. Agree who files the notification and request the Certificate of Acceptance after ESA review.

Read the current ESA notification and inspection guidance, contractor hiring guidance. You can verify an electrical contractor with ESA before agreeing to work.

Questions before you agree to work

Can the household keep living here while each room is rewired in turn?

Some households do, but it needs a plan for the circuits that stay on. The contractor can set out, stage by stage, which rooms keep power and where temporary arrangements are needed.

How do I keep other trades from cutting circuits that serve occupied rooms?

Name the contractor as the single contact for anything electrical and share the stage plan with every trade. Ask that circuits serving occupied rooms be labelled so no one disconnects them by mistake.

Prepare your enquiry

Useful project details

  • Drawings and intended use of each space
  • Appliance and equipment model information
  • Construction schedule and other trades
  • Existing installation records if available
  • Person authorized to approve changes

Only photograph labels and locations that you can see safely. Do not remove electrical covers or approach damaged equipment to gather information.
