A symptom is a starting point, not a diagnosis

Loss of power, flickering, recurring protective-device trips or a damaged device can have different causes. The useful information is what happened, where and when, not a guess made from equipment age. A Licensed Electrical Contractor should assess the installation within a defined diagnostic scope and explain the identified cause before repair work is approved.

Prepare the scope for your Thornhill property

Record which appliances were running together when the breaker tripped, which room they are in and how often it happens. Leave a repeatedly tripping breaker alone rather than resetting it, and never remove the panel cover.

Ask the contractor which loads the assessment will separate and in what order, so that the rooms the household relies on stay in use where that is practical. Ask what written finding will follow.

What should the assessment establish?

The report records which circuits and devices in the Thornhill house were involved and under what combination of appliances the breaker tripped. A short section gives the contractor's account of what was examined, what could not be reached and what was not tested. Its finding separates an overloaded circuit from a damaged device or connection, a view reached through examination rather than assumed beforehand. For the household, a note names the parts of the house that can stay in use meanwhile and anything to be left switched off. Any repair is described apart from the diagnosis, in its own section.

Ontario electrical safety

ESA says almost all electrical work needs a notification of work. If you hire someone for home electrical work, use a Licensed Electrical Contractor. Agree who files the notification and request the Certificate of Acceptance after ESA review.

Read the current ESA notification and inspection guidance, contractor hiring guidance. You can verify an electrical contractor with ESA before agreeing to work.

Questions before you agree to work

Can I keep using the other rooms while a breaker keeps tripping?

Rooms on other circuits may stay usable, but that depends on what is behind the trips. Stop using any device that shows heat, a burning smell or buzzing, leave it alone, and ask the contractor what should stay off.

Does tripping with two appliances together mean the wiring is faulty?

Not necessarily. A circuit can reach its limit when two loads share it, and a worn device can trip a breaker too. Only an assessment by a Licensed Electrical Contractor can tell which applies in your house.
