Electrical safety assessment in Thornhill.

An electrical safety assessment of a Thornhill home can only cover what the owner controls, which in a shared building may be the unit alone. Knowing that boundary before you send an enquiry keeps the written result honest about what was reviewed.

A useful assessment has a stated purpose and boundary

A condition concern, renovation decision or insurer request can each call for a different assessment. Agree which equipment and areas will be reviewed, whether testing is included and what written result will be provided. Concealed wiring and inaccessible equipment may limit conclusions. A general visit should not be represented as complete certification of everything in the property.

Prepare the scope for your Thornhill property

Write down which parts of the building are yours and which belong to a condominium corporation or landlord, and find the unit's own panel position. Gather the purchase inspection report and any earlier electrical paperwork.

Ask the contractor which equipment inside the unit is included and what happens if a finding points to something shared. Ask how the visit is arranged so rooms not under review stay in use.

What should the assessment establish?

Going ahead with any work in a Thornhill unit of a shared building, and when, needs a report clear about its reach. It states plainly that only the unit was reviewed and lists the rooms and equipment included, never describing the whole building as reviewed. What was tested appears beside what could not be reached or switched off. Anything that seems to involve common equipment is reported to you in writing, so it can be passed to the condominium corporation or landlord. The report also notes which parts of the unit stayed in use during the visit and which were set aside.

Ontario electrical safety

ESA says almost all electrical work needs a notification of work. If you hire someone for home electrical work, use a Licensed Electrical Contractor. Agree who files the notification and request the Certificate of Acceptance after ESA review.

Read the current ESA notification and inspection guidance, contractor hiring guidance. You can verify an electrical contractor with ESA before agreeing to work.

Questions before you agree to work

Can the assessment cover the hallway panel or only my own unit?

That depends on who controls the equipment. A condominium corporation or landlord usually decides on common areas, so the contractor may only review your unit unless they give permission in writing.

Will a unit-only review satisfy the insurer or the lender?

Check the exact wording the insurer or lender asks for before ordering anything. A unit-only review may not meet a request for the whole building, so send their letter with your enquiry.
